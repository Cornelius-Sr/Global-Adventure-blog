Stories from the road

My Top 3 Cities in the World

Three cities, countless memories: the places that captured our hearts and refused to let go.

Start the journey

Why These Three?

We are a group of friends who share one obsession: getting lost in new places. After years of passport stamps, missed trains and unforgettable meals, we sat down and asked each other a hard question: which cities would we go back to tomorrow?

This blog is our digital scrapbook. For each city you will find the sights we loved, the food we still dream about, the culture that surprised us, and the small moments that made each place ours.

The Lineup

  1. Tokyo, Japan Neon, tradition and perfect precision
  2. Lisbon, Portugal Hills, tiles and golden light
  3. Marrakech, Morocco Spice, color and sensory overload

No. 1

Tokyo, Japan

Where the future and the past share every street corner

  • Country: Japan
  • Best time to visit: Spring and autumn
  • Written by: The travel team

Our Story

Tokyo introduced itself in layers: the calm rhythm of the train ride from the airport, lantern-lit lanes in Asakusa, and then the bright rush of Shibuya after dark. Every turn seemed to offer a different version of the city, yet the trains, signs and tiny neighborhood shops made it all feel remarkably easy to explore.

One evening we ducked into a little ramen shop to escape the rain. We left warm, full and already planning which neighborhood to explore the next morning. That mix of energy and everyday kindness is what made Tokyo feel so welcoming.

Must-See Attractions

  • Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa

    Pass through the huge red lantern at Kaminarimon and follow Nakamise Street toward this centuries-old Buddhist temple. Go early for a quieter visit, then take time to explore the surrounding neighborhood.

  • Shibuya Crossing

    Hundreds of people cross at once beneath a wall of glowing screens. Find a spot overlooking the intersection, then join the flow and experience the city's famous pace from street level.

  • Meiji Shrine

    A broad, peaceful path leads through a forested park to this Shinto shrine. The quiet walk is a welcome contrast to nearby Harajuku and a lovely place to slow down.

Culinary Delights

Tokyo rewards curiosity at every meal, from a quick bowl at a station counter to carefully prepared sushi. Follow the locals, be ready to queue, and don't overlook the small neighborhood spots.

Ramen
A rich broth, springy noodles and a perfectly seasoned egg make a memorable bowl. Look for a well-reviewed ramen shop in Shinjuku or try a local favorite near your stay.
Sushi
For fresh, seasonal sushi, visit a neighborhood sushi bar or the outer market area near Tsukiji. Choose a busy counter and enjoy the chef's recommendations.
Street snacks
Try taiyaki, grilled snacks or sweet mochi from a market stall. Nakamise Street is an easy place to sample traditional treats while walking toward Senso-ji.

Culture and Character

Tokyo's character comes from its contrasts: quiet shrine courtyards sit minutes from neon arcades, while careful everyday manners help busy public spaces run smoothly. A small bow, patient queueing and keeping phone calls quiet on trains go a long way.

In Tokyo, even a short walk can feel like a journey between different worlds.

No. 2

Lisbon, Portugal

A city of seven hills, golden light and effortless charm

  • Country: Portugal
  • Best time to visit: Late spring and early autumn
  • Written by: The travel team

Our Story

Lisbon won us over at walking pace. We climbed a steep lane past tiled facades, heard a tram rattle around a corner and reached a viewpoint just as the rooftops turned gold in the evening light. The city felt lived-in and relaxed, with a new view waiting at the top of every hill.

We took a wrong turn in Alfama and found a tiny bakery instead of the museum we were looking for. A warm pastel de nata and a few minutes watching the neighborhood go by turned the detour into one of our favorite afternoons.

Must-See Attractions

  • Tram 28

    Ride this historic yellow tram through narrow streets and several of Lisbon's best-known neighborhoods. It gets busy, so travel early or take another route uphill and enjoy the tram from the street.

  • Belem Tower

    This riverside fortress recalls Lisbon's seafaring past, with detailed stonework and broad views over the Tagus. Pair it with a walk along the waterfront in Belém.

  • Alfama District

    Wander through steep lanes, tiled buildings and small squares in one of Lisbon's oldest districts. The best moments often come between landmarks, when you pause for a view or hear fado drifting from a doorway.

Culinary Delights

Lisbon's food is made for lingering: simple seafood, comforting classics and pastries best enjoyed with coffee. Try neighborhood tasquinhas and bakeries, and save room for one more custard tart.

Pastel de nata
This crisp, flaky custard tart is best eaten warm with a dusting of cinnamon. Belém is famous for its version, but excellent pastéis de nata can be found throughout the city.
Grilled sardines
Charcoal-grilled sardines are a Lisbon staple, especially during summer festivals. Look for them at a traditional neighborhood restaurant when they are in season.
Bacalhau
Portugal's much-loved salt cod appears in countless recipes, from baked dishes to crisp bolinhos. Ask what the kitchen recommends and share a few plates.

Culture and Character

Lisbon's personality is written into its azulejo tiles, heard in the melancholy melodies of fado and felt in its easygoing cafe culture. Respectful listening makes a fado performance more meaningful, while local festivals bring music and grilled sardines into the streets.

In Lisbon, the scenic route is often the best route.

No. 3

Marrakech, Morocco

A maze of color, spice and unforgettable sound

  • Country: Morocco
  • Best time to visit: Spring and autumn
  • Written by: The travel team

Our Story

Marrakech met us with warm air, winding streets and the layered sounds of the medina. One moment we were following the scent of spices through a souk; the next, we had stepped into a quiet courtyard shaded by orange trees. The city asks you to pay attention, and rewards you at every turn.

On our first evening we watched Jemaa el-Fnaa fill with food stalls as the sun went down. We shared a few small plates, listened to the square come alive and realized that in Marrakech, the atmosphere is part of the meal.

Must-See Attractions

  • Jemaa el-Fnaa

    As daylight fades, the square transforms into a lively gathering place filled with food stalls, music and conversation. Take in the scene from a terrace, then choose a busy stall for a freshly cooked meal.

  • Majorelle Garden

    Discover vivid blue walls, fountains and a collection of plants in this peaceful garden. Arrive early if you can, and reserve time to enjoy the contrast with the busy streets outside.

  • Bahia Palace

    Explore elegant courtyards, carved cedar and intricate tilework in this historic palace. Look closely at the craftsmanship and imagine the many lives that once filled its rooms.

Culinary Delights

Marrakech's food draws on fragrant spices, slow cooking and generous hospitality. We loved sharing dishes at a small table, sipping mint tea after a busy walk and sampling just one more thing from the market.

Tagine
Slow-cooked in an earthenware pot, tagine brings meat or vegetables together with warm spices and tender sauce. Try one at a well-reviewed local restaurant, with bread for scooping up every last bite.
Mint tea
Fresh mint tea is often poured from a height and shared as a gesture of welcome. Take a seat at a cafe or riad courtyard and enjoy it slowly.
Souk street food
Follow the aromas through the souks and look for freshly cooked breads, grilled skewers or seasonal snacks. Choose a busy stall, check prices before ordering and ask before taking photographs of vendors.

Culture and Character

Handmade leather, woven textiles, brass lamps and cedar carvings show the skill of Marrakech's artisans. In the souks, friendly bargaining is common; keep it respectful, agree on a price before buying and ask permission before photographing people. The call to prayer and the rhythms of Gnawa music are part of the city's rich cultural soundscape.

The best way to experience the medina is to leave room for a few wrong turns.
